Trading Strategy Instructions:

Objective: Develop a trading strategy based on moving average crossovers.

Strategy Name: Moving Average Crossover Strategy

Financial Instruments: This strategy applies to any liquid and frequently traded financial instrument (e.g., stocks, ETFs, forex).

Time Frame: Daily charts

Indicators:
1. Short-term Moving Average (SMA1): 50-day simple moving average
2. Long-term Moving Average (SMA2): 200-day simple moving average

Entry Conditions:
1. Buy Signal: A buy signal occurs when the short-term moving average (SMA1) crosses above the long-term moving average (SMA2). This is known as a "Golden Cross."
   - The close of the crossover day is the reference point.
   - Execute a buy order at the opening price on the next trading day following the crossover.
   
2. Sell Signal: A sell signal occurs when the short-term moving average (SMA1) crosses below the long-term moving average (SMA2). This is known as a "Death Cross."
   - The close of the crossover day is the reference point.
   - Execute a sell order at the opening price on the next trading day following the crossover.

Position Sizing:
- Risk 1% of the trading capital per trade.
- Adjust the position size accordingly based on the trading capital and the opening price at which the trade is executed.

Stop Loss and Take Profit:
- No stop loss or take profit is set for this strategy. Positions are closed based on the opposite crossover signal.

Exit Conditions:
1. Close Buy Position: When a sell signal occurs (SMA1 crosses below SMA2), close the buy position at the opening price on the next trading day.
2. Close Sell Position: When a buy signal occurs (SMA1 crosses above SMA2), close the sell position at the opening price on the next trading day.

Risk Management:
- If there is already an open position in the direction of the trade signal, no new trades should be executed.
- If the capital drops below a predefined threshold, say 20% of initial capital, halt trading.
- Do not take additional trades if there are insufficient funds to risk 1% of trading capital.

Backtest Parameters:
- Backtest period: Minimum of 5 years historical data.
- Include trading fees and slippage in the backtest.
- Report metrics: Total return, annualized return, maximum drawdown, Sharpe ratio, and number of trades.

Coding the Backtest:
- Use historical daily opening and closing prices for backtesting.
- Calculate daily 50-day and 200-day simple moving averages.
- Implement logic to detect crossovers and execute trades accordingly.
- Ensure trades occur at the first opening price following the signal.
- Maintain a log of all trades, including entry and exit points, trade duration, and profit or loss.
- Calculate the required backtest metrics after completing the simulation.
