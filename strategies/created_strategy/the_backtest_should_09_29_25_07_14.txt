Trading Strategy Instructions:

1. Strategy Overview:
The trading strategy is predicated on mean reversion, specifically focusing on the Bollinger Bands and the relative strength index (RSI) as primary indicators for identifying overbought or oversold conditions.

2. Instruments to Trade:
The backtest should be conducted on a highly liquid financial instrument such as a major Forex pair (e.g., EUR/USD), a major index (e.g., S&P 500 ETF), or a blue-chip stock.

3. Timeframe:
The primary timeframe for analysis is the daily chart. However, for entry confirmation, the 4-hour chart will be used.

4. Technical Indicators:
   - Bollinger Bands (20 periods, 2 standard deviations)
   - Relative Strength Index (RSI) with a period of 14 days

5. Entry Conditions:
   - For a Long Position (Buy): Enter a trade when the price closes below the lower Bollinger Band and the RSI is below 30. This indicates an oversold condition.
   - For a Short Position (Sell): Enter a trade when the price closes above the upper Bollinger Band and the RSI is above 70. This indicates an overbought condition.

   - Confirmation: After meeting the above conditions on the daily chart, wait for a reversal candlestick pattern (e.g., hammer, bullish engulfing for long entry, and shooting star, bearish engulfing for short entry) on the 4-hour chart to confirm the trade entry.

6. Exit Conditions:
   - For a Long Position: Exit the trade when the price reaches the middle Bollinger Band (20-period moving average) or after 5 trading days, whichever comes first.
   - For a Short Position: Exit the trade when the price reaches the middle Bollinger Band (20-period moving average) or after 5 trading days, whichever comes first.
   - Stop Loss: Set a stop loss at the recent swing high/low plus a buffer (e.g., 1% of the swing price for stocks, 20 pips for Forex).

7. Position Sizing:
Determine the position size based on a fixed percentage of the total portfolio at risk per trade (e.g., 1%).

8. Risk Management:
Set a maximum drawdown limit for the entire portfolio (e.g., 20%). If the portfolio's value drops by this percentage from the peak, halt trading and re-evaluate the strategy.

9. Additional Rules:
   - Do not enter a new trade if there is already an open position for the same instrument.
   - Do not enter trades before major economic announcements that can increase volatility.

10. Backtesting Parameters:
   - Backtest the strategy over at least five years of historical price data to ensure robustness across different market conditions.
   - Record the trades, including entry price, exit price, position size, stop loss level, and the trade outcome (profit/loss).
   - Calculate performance metrics such as the total return, average return per trade, win rate, maximum drawdown, and Sharpe ratio.

Coding Instructions:
1. Pull historical daily and 4-hour chart price data for the selected instrument.
2. Implement the Bollinger Bands and RSI calculations using the specified parameters.

4. Code the exit conditions including the logic for stop loss and taking profit.
5. Implement the position sizing and risk management rules.
6. Perform the backtest across the historical dataset and log all trades.
7. Calculate and report performance metrics after backtesting is complete.
8. Ensure that the backtesting framework can generate a detailed report and equity curve for visual analysis.
