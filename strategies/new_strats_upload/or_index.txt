Trading Strategy: Mean Reversion with Bollinger Bands

Objective:
Develop a mean reversion trading strategy that uses Bollinger Bands to identify entry and exit points for trades. The strategy should be backtested on daily price data for a specified stock or index.

Entry Criteria:
1. Calculate the 20-day Simple Moving Average (SMA) of the closing prices.
2. Calculate the upper and lower Bollinger Bands with a 2 standard deviation offset from the SMA.
3. A long entry signal is generated when the daily closing price crosses below the lower Bollinger Band, suggesting an oversold condition.
4. A short entry signal is generated when the daily closing price crosses above the upper Bollinger Band, suggesting an overbought condition.

Exit Criteria:
1. For long positions, exit when the price touches or crosses back above the SMA.
2. For short positions, exit when the price touches or crosses back below the SMA.
3. Additionally, implement a stop-loss at 3% below the entry price for long trades and 3% above the entry price for short trades.
4. Implement a time-based exit where positions are closed after 10 trading days if none of the other exit criteria have been met.

Position Sizing:
1. Risk 1% of the total capital on each trade.
2. Determine the number of shares to trade by dividing the risk amount by the distance from the entry price to the stop-loss level.

Risk Management:
1. The maximum number of concurrent trades should be limited to 5 to diversify risk.
2. If a trade is closed based on the stop-loss criteria, do not open a new trade on the same stock until 5 trading days have passed to avoid catching a "falling knife".

Backtest Specifications:
1. Backtest period: 5 years of historical daily price data.
2. Starting capital: $100,000.
3. Transaction costs: Assume $0.01 per share for commission.
4. Slippage: Assume a slippage of 0.05% of the execution price.
5. Data points: Record the entry date, entry price, exit date, exit price, position size, and P&L for each trade.

Performance Metrics:
1. Calculate the Net Profit/Loss.
2. Calculate the Percentage of Winning Trades.
3. Calculate the Average Gain for Winning Trades and Average Loss for Losing Trades.
4. Calculate the Maximum Drawdown.
5. Calculate the Annualized Return and the Sharpe Ratio.

Backtesting Implementation Steps for the Other AI:
1. Import historical daily price data for the specified stock/index for the backtest period.
2. Calculate the SMA, upper band, and lower band for each trading day.
3. Implement the trading logic for entry and exit conditions, including the position-sizing and risk management rules.
4. Execute trades according to the strategy rules and record trade details.
5. At the end of the backtest period, calculate the performance metrics.
6. Present the results including a chart of the equity curve over time.

Important Notes:
- All entry signals should occur on the close of the trading day with trades executed at the next day's open price.
- Adjust the stop-loss level for each new day based on the closing price.
- Recapitalize profits and losses daily for compounding effects.
- Report any deviations from expected results or potential issues encountered during backtesting.
