Trading Strategy Instructions for AI to Code the Backtest:

Strategy Name: Dual Moving Average Crossover Strategy with Risk Management

Objective: To create a dual moving average crossover system that triggers buy and sell signals, alongside implementing risk management techniques to optimize the strategy's performance and minimize potential drawdowns.

Strategy Details:
1. Selection of Financial Instrument:
   - Choose a liquid financial instrument that exhibits clear trends. This strategy applies to stocks, forex, commodities, or indices.

2. Time Frame for Analysis:
   - Utilize daily time frames for signal generation.

3. Indicators and Parameters:
   - Use two Simple Moving Averages (SMAs) with the following periods:
     - Short-term SMA: 50-day period
     - Long-term SMA: 200-day period

4. Entry Conditions:
   - Buy Signal: Generate a buy signal when the 50-day SMA crosses above the 200-day SMA (Golden Cross).
   - Sell Signal: Generate a sell signal when the 50-day SMA crosses below the 200-day SMA (Death Cross).

5. Exit Conditions:
   - Close a buy position when the 50-day SMA crosses below the 200-day SMA.
   - Close a sell position when the 50-day SMA crosses above the 200-day SMA.

6. Position Sizing and Risk Management:
   - Determine the maximum allowable risk per trade, typically between 1% - 2% of the trading account balance.
   - Calculate the position size based on the stop-loss level and the maximum allowable risk. The position size = (Account Balance * Max Risk) / Stop Loss in price units.
   - Place stop-loss orders to limit losses. The initial stop-loss should be set at a previous swing high or low, with a buffer (e.g., a percentage of the Average True Range (ATR)).

7. Trailing Stop-Loss:
   - Once a trade becomes profitable, employ a trailing stop-loss to protect gains and enable the position to capture further upside. The trailing stop can be a fixed dollar amount or a percentage below the highest price achieved since entry.

8. Additional Risk Controls:
   - Implement a maximum drawdown threshold for the overall portfolio. If the drawdown exceeds this value, halt trading temporarily.
   - Include provisions for skipping the next trade if the previous trade was a significant loss, in order to prevent emotional trading and over-leveraging.

9. Backtesting Criteria:
   - Backtest the strategy over at least five years of historical data to capture different market conditions.
   - Evaluate performance metrics like total return, maximum drawdown, win rate, and Sharpe ratio.
   - Consider transaction costs, slippage, and potential impact on results.

Instructions for Other AI to Build the Backtest:

1. Import historical daily price data for the chosen financial instrument spanning a minimum of five years.

2. Calculate the 50-day and 200-day SMAs for the entire data set.

3. Identify all crossover points where either the 50-day SMA crosses above or below the 200-day SMA.

4. Simulate entry and exit trades based on the crossover points while adhering to the position sizing and risk management rules.

5. Set initial stop-loss levels for each trade and adjust for trailing stop-losses as per instructions.

6. Record each trade's entry, exit, position size, profit/loss, and any drawdown experienced.

7. After completing the backtest, aggregate the trades to compute total return, maximum drawdown, win rate, and Sharpe ratio, taking into account transaction costs and slippage.

8. Implement the maximum drawdown threshold logic to halt trading when necessary.

9. Execute the backtest code ensuring that there are no lookahead biases, meaning that future information is not used in historical trading decisions.

Your output will be a detailed backtesting report that covers the trading strategy's performance, consistency, and risks, providing an evidence-based assessment of the strategy's viability in historical market conditions.
